## Methodology

> **This is a SYNTHETIC SIMULATION. The temperatures, indices and land-cover maps are generated by
> mathematical models in MATLAB. They are NOT satellite-derived Land Surface Temperature (LST) and do
> not describe any real place.**

The project is a controlled numerical experiment: because the data-generating process is known, the
analysis can be checked against the truth that produced it.

## 1. Synthetic landscape generation

The study area is an `N × N` grid (default `N = 500`, cell size `Δ = 10 m`, i.e. 5 km × 5 km). Cell
coordinates are normalised to `(x, y) ∈ [0, 1]²`. Every land-cover class is derived from smooth
*driver fields* so that patches are connected and irregular rather than random pixels.

## 2. Spatial pattern generation

Spatially correlated noise is produced by spectral synthesis: white Gaussian noise is filtered in the
Fourier domain with amplitude `|k|^(-β/2)`, i.e. a power spectrum `P(k) ∝ |k|^(-β)`, and then
z-scored:

$$
\eta_\beta(\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{ |\mathbf{k}|^{-\beta/2}\, \mathcal{F}\{ w(\mathbf{x}) \} \right\}, \qquad w \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)
$$

Larger `β` gives larger, smoother blobs (`β ≈ 3` for vegetation and lake shape, `β ≈ 1.4–1.6` for
sensor-like texture). White noise is drawn from a deterministic Park–Miller generator with a
Box–Muller transform, which makes results identical on all platforms.

## 3. Built-up model

Urban intensity is the sum of Gaussian cores and Gaussian-profile corridors (distance to a line
segment) plus texture, then smoothed and rescaled to `[0, 1]`:

$$
U(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i w_i \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert \mathbf{x}-\mathbf{c}_i \rVert^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)
 + \sum_j v_j \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_j(\mathbf{x})^2}{2h_j^2}\right) + \alpha\,\eta_{2.6}(\mathbf{x})
$$

Built-up cells are the non-water cells with `U` above the percentile that yields the configured
built-up fraction (default 26 % of land).

## 4. Water model

Water = river ∪ lake.

- **River:** centre line `y_c(x) = y_0 + 0.10 sin(2π·1.3x + 1) + 0.04 sin(2π·3.1x + 2)`, with a
  variable half-width; a cell belongs to the river if `|y − y_c(x)| < half-width(x)`.
- **Lake:** `exp(−r²/2σ²) + γ η_3(x) > θ`, a Gaussian bump with a noise-perturbed, irregular shoreline.

## 5. Vegetation model and bare soil

Among the remaining land, vegetation potential is
`V = 0.75 η_3 − 0.90 U` (vegetation avoids the urban core). Bare soil is chosen from
`B = η_{2.6} + 0.8 U` (patchy, more common on the urban fringe). Vegetated cells are split into dense
and sparse vegetation at a percentile of `V`. Classes: 1 water, 2 dense vegetation, 3 sparse
vegetation, 4 built-up, 5 bare soil.

## 6. Spectral indices

Class indicator maps `I_k` are Gaussian-blurred (σ = 1.2 cells) to obtain sub-pixel fractions
`f_k^opt`, mimicking sensor mixing. Each index is a fraction-weighted mean of class-typical values
plus independent correlated noise:

$$
\mathrm{NDVI} = \sum_k f_k^{\mathrm{opt}}\,\mu_k^{\mathrm{NDVI}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{NDVI}}
$$

and likewise for NDBI and NDWI. Class-typical values (water, dense, sparse, built-up, bare):

| Index | Water | Dense veg. | Sparse veg. | Built-up | Bare soil |
|-------|------:|-----------:|------------:|---------:|----------:|
| NDVI | −0.25 | 0.78 | 0.42 | 0.10 | 0.15 |
| NDBI | −0.30 | −0.38 | −0.16 | 0.28 | 0.12 |
| NDWI | 0.55 | −0.50 | −0.30 | −0.20 | −0.25 |

Noise (SD 0.05) is independent of the LST noise, so index–LST relationships arise only through the
shared landscape.

## 7. LST equation

LST is **not** assigned per class. It is an additive model of physical drivers (°C), using
thermally-blurred fractions `f_k` (σ = 3 cells):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{LST} ={}& T_0 + a_b f_{\mathrm{built}} + a_s f_{\mathrm{soil}} - a_{dv} f_{\mathrm{dense}} - a_{sv} f_{\mathrm{sparse}} - a_w f_{\mathrm{water}} \\
& - c_w\, e^{-d_w/L_w}\,(1-f_{\mathrm{water}}) - c_v\, e^{-d_v/L_v}\,(1-f_{\mathrm{dense}}) \\
& + \mathrm{UHI} + G(x,y) + \epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

| Term | Meaning | Default |
|------|---------|--------:|
| `T0` | Reference temperature of open land | 31 °C |
| `a_b`, `a_s` | Warming by built-up / bare soil | 7, 4 °C |
| `a_dv`, `a_sv` | Cooling by dense / sparse vegetation | 6, 2.5 °C |
| `a_w` | Local cooling by water | 8 °C |
| `c_w`, `L_w` | Water cooling halo amplitude and decay length | 3 °C, 250 m |
| `c_v`, `L_v` | Vegetation cooling halo amplitude and decay length | 1.5 °C, 150 m |
| `UHI` | `2.5 · G_σ * U` (smoothed urban intensity, σ = 15 cells) | 2.5 °C |
| `G(x,y)` | Linear regional gradient | 1.2 (W→E), −0.8 (S→N) °C |
| `ε` | Spatially correlated noise, `0.7 · η_1.6` | SD 0.7 °C |

Every term is stored in the `lst_components` structure of the results file so that contributions can
be inspected separately.

## 8. Spatial cooling effect and distance-zone analysis

The exact Euclidean distance to the nearest water cell (`d_w`) and nearest dense-vegetation cell
(`d_v`) is computed with a separable distance transform (Felzenszwalb & Huttenlocher, 2012) written in
base MATLAB. Zones are `(lo, hi]` in metres: 0–50, 50–100, 100–200, 200–300, 300–500 and
500–1000. For each zone the mean, SD, standard error and cell count of LST are reported.

- Water profile: all land cells (water cells themselves are excluded, since they are trivially cold).
- Vegetation profile: cells that are neither water nor dense vegetation.

The profile "warming" is `mean(LST_last zone) − mean(LST_first zone)`, and a Pearson correlation
between distance and LST is reported. Profiles are correlational and inherit confounding from the
landscape layout.

## 9. Correlation analysis

Pearson's correlation between each index and LST over all `n = N²` cells:

$$
r = \frac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2 \sum (y_i-\bar y)^2}}, \qquad
t = r\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{1-r^2}}
$$

The two-sided p-value uses the incomplete beta function, `p = I_{ν/(ν+t²)}(ν/2, 1/2)` with `ν = n − 2`,
so no Statistics Toolbox is needed. With `n = 250 000` cells, even tiny correlations can be
"significant"; spatial autocorrelation also means the effective sample size is much smaller, so p-values
should be read as descriptive.

## 10. Regression model

$$
\mathrm{LST}_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{NDVI}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{NDBI}_i + \beta_3\,\mathrm{NDWI}_i + e_i
$$

Ordinary least squares, `β̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹ Xᵀ y` (`X \ y` in MATLAB). Reported quantities:

$$
R^2 = 1-\frac{SSE}{SST}, \qquad \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{SSE/n}, \qquad
\mathrm{SE}(\hat\beta_j)=\sqrt{\hat\sigma^2\,[(X^\top X)^{-1}]_{jj}},\quad \hat\sigma^2=\frac{SSE}{n-4}
$$

and t-statistics `β̂_j / SE_j` with `n − 4` degrees of freedom. Standardised coefficients are also
stored. NDVI, NDBI and NDWI are correlated (multicollinearity), so individual coefficients should be
interpreted cautiously; the regression is a demonstration, not a causal model.

## 11. Reproducibility

All random fields come from a deterministic generator seeded from `cfg.seed = 42` (`rng(42)` is also
called). Outputs are therefore identical across runs, MATLAB releases and operating systems.
